Working in Confluence

What belongs here rather than in Linear, which kind of page to write, and how to make it findable six months later.

Confluence or Linear?

The question isn't how big it is. It's how long it stays true.

Something to be done → Linear.
Something we now know → Confluence.

Linear

Work

Has an owner, a status, and an end.

  • Tasks, bugs, decisions to make
  • Interesting until it's Done
  • Then it's history

Confluence

Knowledge

Still useful after the work is finished.

  • What we decided and why
  • What we found out
  • How to do it again

The test

Name who reads it in three months and what they get from it. If you can't, it's an issue description or a comment — not a page.

Decide pilot pricing is a Linear issue. The pricing we landed on, and why, is a page. Never copy an issue into a page just to have a record; link to it.

Page types

Five. If it fits none of them, ask before inventing a sixth.

TypeHoldsStays true because
DecisionWhat we chose, what we rejected, whyIt records a moment — never edited to a different answer
SpecWhat we're building, what done looks likeWritten before the build, closed out after
ResearchWhat we found outEvery claim names its source
RunbookHow to do a repeatable thingWhoever changes the process updates it
Meeting notesSaid, decided, assignedDated, never rewritten

Decisions are the ones people skip

They're also the highest value. Any time an argument gets settled, that's a Decision page — including the options you turned down, which is the part anyone comes back for. Without it the same argument runs again in six months, from scratch.

TypeOwner + review date?
Runbook · SpecYes — they describe how things are, so they go quietly wrong when reality moves
Decision · Meeting notesNo — records of a moment can't expire
ResearchNo — the date is in the title instead

Where it goes

By venture, not by team. Linear routes by team because teams decide how work gets done — but a finished page is knowledge about something, and everything known about one venture belongs together no matter who wrote it.

A venture gets its own space once its Linear Project is actually started. Until then it's an idea, and lives in the house space.

The same five trees everywhere

Decisions/ Specs/ Research/ Runbooks/ Meetings/ — in the house space, in each venture space, and inside each idea. Pick the type and the location follows; there's no judgement call.

Subject is a label, not a tree. Reuse the words already on Business issues in Linear — product, design, research, fundraising, finance, legal, ops — plus whatever it's actually about (pricing, onboarding). One vocabulary across both tools beats two.

Graduation

When a Project starts but its pages are still under Ideas/, Claude will say so and offer to move them. You create the space — that sets a key and permissions and can't be renamed cleanly later. Claude then moves the tree and re-points any page links attached to Linear issues, so nothing dangles.

Writing one

Search first. If a page on this already exists, edit that one — two pages disagreeing about the same subject is how a wiki dies.

Someone who wasn't in the room should be able to read it and act without asking you what you meant.

  1. Title it for search

    People type what they want to know, not what you called it.

    • Decisions state the decision — Charge per seat, not per invoice, not Pricing model. The title alone should answer it.
    • Date anything that's a snapshot — notes, research. Never "current" or "latest"; both are wrong within the month.
    • Subject, not ceremony. Meeting notes 3 tells nobody anything.

  2. Open with what it's for

    One or two lines, at the top, saying what this is and who it's for. Someone arriving from search decides in about five seconds whether to keep reading.

  3. Then write the type

    • Decision — what we decided · what we rejected and why · what would make us revisit it
    • Spec — the problem in the reader's terms · what done looks like · decisions already made · what's deliberately out of scope
    • Research — the question · what you found · a source for every claim. Unsourced research is an opinion page.
    • Runbook — numbered steps someone can follow without you · what to do when a step fails
    • Meeting notes — date · who was there · what was decided · who owns what next

  4. Runbook or Spec? Add an owner

    A line at the very top — Owner: <name> Review by: <~3 months>. The owner is whoever would have to fix it, not whoever wrote it. Other types don't get one.

  5. Keep it scannable

    Short sections and tables over long prose. Plain language, same as everywhere else. These pages get scanned for one answer, not read start to finish.

Linking to Linear

Pages and issues answer different questions, so they should always point at each other.

  • Put the issue key in the page — ENG-123, BIZ-45 — and paste the link. Linear picks up the mention, so the connection shows from both sides.
  • On the issue, paste the page link into Context, where Linear renders a preview, or attach it with the page's title.
  • A Spec names the issues that implement it. A Decision names the issue that triggered it.

Changing pages

A wiki people don't trust is worse than no wiki.

Fix what's wrong

If a page is out of date and you know the current answer, correct it. If you don't know the answer, say on the page that it's stale rather than leaving a confident, wrong page in place.

Except records

Never rewrite a Decision or Meeting notes page to say something different. They record a moment. When the answer changes, write a new Decision and link the old one as superseded — the history of the thinking is the point.

Deleting

Out of dateArchive it, or mark it superseded and link what replaced it
JunkAccident, test page, empty draft — delete it

Never delete a page for being old. Old and correct is the entire point.

Keep in mind

The skills repo is public. It describes how we work, which is fine to share. Never put target markets, customer names, pricing or strategy in it. What you write inside a Confluence page is private.

If the way we work changes, this page and the skills change with it. When they disagree, the skills are what's actually running.
