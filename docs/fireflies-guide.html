Working with Fireflies

How a recorded meeting becomes a page in the Brain, why those pages carry no names, and how to ask questions about what was said.

What it is

Fireflies is the notetaker bot in our calls. This plugin is how its recordings become knowledge instead of a pile of links.

Fireflies joins a meeting, records it, and produces a transcript and an automatic summary. One account covers the whole team — the bot joins whenever the account holder is in the call, so nobody else needs a seat.

The plugin does two things with a recording:

  • File it — write a full meeting-notes page into the right space's Meetings tree in Confluence, in the fixed house template.
  • Ask about it — answer questions in chat, straight from the transcript, without writing anything.

Getting connected

Once per person. No credentials live in the repo.

/plugin marketplace add E-D-E-A/workflow-skills
/plugin install edea-fireflies@edea

The plugin ships the official Fireflies connector. The first time a skill uses it, a browser window opens to sign in to the Fireflies account — after that it just works.

Filing pages also needs edea-confluence installed — the Fireflies skills build on its rules rather than carrying their own copy. If it's missing, Claude will stop and say so.

The nameless rule

The one rule that makes these pages trustworthy.

Fireflies can't reliably tell who is speaking. What was said is solid; who said it is a guess. So a page built from a recording carries no names in its body.

We've watched the speaker labels swap people mid-conversation on our own meetings. A misattributed opinion in a permanent record is worse than no attribution — so the summary phrases everything as the room: "the team agreed", "one view was… the counter was…".

  • Participants still appear — once, in the meeting-details table. That list comes from the calendar invite, not from voices, so it's reliable.
  • Action items are unowned by default. An owner is written only when a person supplies it — Claude asks at filing time; it never reads owners out of the transcript.
  • "Who said that?" gets an honest answer: the transcript can't be trusted on that, here's what was said. The recording link is in every page for anyone who wants to listen.

Filing a meeting

Say it in your own words — "summarize this meeting to Confluence", "create a wiki from the conversation", "add yesterday's call to the Brain" — with a Fireflies link if you have one.

  1. Claude finds the meeting

    From the link you pasted, or by searching by date — "yesterday's call" works. If more than one matches, it lists candidates and asks.

  2. Reads all of it

    The full transcript plus the automatic summary, end to end, before writing a word. Nothing important gets dropped because it happened in minute 90.

  3. Asks three questions

    • Which space? — the page goes under that space's Meetings tree.
    • Hebrew or English? — the language the meeting was held in decides nothing.
    • Task owners? — name who owns what, or leave the action items unowned.

  4. Writes and checks

    The eight-section template below, then a separate pass over the draft hunting only for names that slipped in. Hebrew pages come out right-to-left automatically.

  5. Files it and reports

    Under the space's Meetings tree, linked to the previous meeting's page. If a page for this meeting already exists, Claude shows it and asks instead of overwriting — meeting notes are records.

What it deliberately doesn't do

  • No automatic summaries — every page is asked for by a person.
  • No raw transcripts in Confluence — the page links the recording instead.
  • No Linear issues from action items unless you ask — that's linear-issue's job.
  • No editing existing meeting pages — records are never rewritten.

The template

Every meeting page — filed from a recording or written by hand — has the same eight sections in the same order. An empty section stays and says so: "no decisions were made" is information.

SectionHolds
Meeting detailsDate and time · duration · participants (from the invite) · recording link · purpose
SummaryThree to five lines for someone who won't read further
Main pointsThe heart of the page — a sub-heading per subject, every point that mattered
DecisionsNumbered. A decision that matters gets its own Decision page, linked
Open questionsRaised but not settled
Action itemsA task list — owners only when a person named them
Next stepsFollow-ups, and the next meeting if one was set
RelatedLinks to the previous meeting's page and anything the conversation pointed at

Asking questions

No page, no filing — just answers from the transcript, in whatever language you asked.

  • "What did we decide about the onboarding flow?"
  • "מה פספסתי בפגישה של אתמול?"
  • "Give me a quick recap of the call — in chat, don't write anything."
  • "Which meeting did the budget come up in?" — searches across meetings.

Answers come from the meeting, not from general knowledge — if something wasn't discussed, Claude says so rather than filling the gap. Timestamps are quoted where they help, so you can jump to that minute of the recording.

The skills

What the plugin installs, and what each piece is for.

SkillJob
fireflies-fileFiles a recorded meeting into the Brain: reads everything, asks space, language and owners, writes the eight-section template with no names in the body, and runs a names pass before publishing.
fireflies-askAnswers questions about meetings in chat — decisions, subjects, action items, quick recaps, and which meeting covered what. Writes nothing.

Both lean on FIREFLIES.md, the shared reference inside the plugin, and fireflies-file builds on the edea-confluence rules rather than duplicating them.

The skills tell Claude how to work; this page tells people. If they ever disagree, the skill is what's actually running — fix the page.
