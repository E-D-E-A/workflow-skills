How a deck gets made

End to end, a first deck takes under an hour — most of it NotebookLM generating.

  1. Ask

    In your own words: "I need a 12-slide deck for a partner meeting next week."

  2. Answer three questions

    Claude won't start without them: who it's for, what they should decide or do, and Hebrew or English. It won't fill any of them in for you — asking in Hebrew doesn't mean a Hebrew deck. Anything else it's missing, it asks about too, one question at a time.

  3. Review the brief

    You get the deck's story before any slides exist — the narrative, one message per slide, what's fact and what's assumption. Cheapest moment to change direction.

  4. Confirm the run sheet

    One short message sums up what's about to be built — language, slide count, format — and asks: generate in NotebookLM now, or take the brief and paste it into a tool yourself? Nothing runs until you answer.

  5. Wait

    NotebookLM takes 15–45 minutes to generate. Claude starts it and tells you; you don't have to sit there.

  6. Get the file — then fix by slide

    A PPTX (or PDF) lands back with you. Small fixes go per slide — "change the title on slide 4" — without regenerating the deck.

Slide rules

The standard every output follows, whichever tool ends up rendering it.

One slide, one message. Every slide answers one management question, and its title states the conclusion, not the topic.

Titles"The current process creates high cost and low scalability" — never "Market" or "Our Product".
HonestyFacts, assumptions and targets are labelled as what they are. A number we don't have is a marked assumption, never invented.
DensityOne main message, one visual, up to 3 supporting points, roughly 40–60 words. More than that splits into two slides.
ColorWhite or deep-navy background, one accent color. More colors only when they mean something.
ChartsComparison → bar · over time → line · conversion → funnel · priorities → 2×2 · summary → KPI cards. Every chart answers a question.
NeverStock photos, generic AI imagery, decorative 3D, dense text blocks. The brief tells the generator to leave them out.

Hebrew & RTL

In a Hebrew deck, direction is part of correctness — a timeline running the wrong way reads as broken.

Every sequence runs right to left

שלב אחרון← שלב 3← שלב 2← שלב 1
  • Titles, text and bullets align right; tables put the lead column on the right.
  • Processes, timelines and funnels start at the far right; arrows point left.
  • Logos, charts and icons are not mirrored.
  • English terms stay only where they're clearer than the Hebrew.

NotebookLM setup

Once per machine. A developer usually does this for you — after it, "make me a deck" just works.

uv tool install "notebooklm-py[browser]"
notebooklm login

login opens a browser for a normal Google sign-in, one time. No passwords are stored in any repo.

Temporary — July 2026

Google renamed NotebookLM to Gemini Notebook, and the released tool doesn't know the new address yet — login waits forever even after you signed in. Until version 0.8.0 is out, install from the project's main branch instead:

uv tool install "notebooklm-py[browser] @ git+https://github.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py.git"

Delete this notice once 0.8.0 ships.

Built on an unofficial tool

NotebookLM has no official API, so generation runs through a community tool that can break whenever Google changes something. When that happens you still get the full brief to paste into NotebookLM yourself — tell a developer, and they'll sort the tool out.

Keep in mind

The skills repo is public. It describes how we make presentations, which is fine to share. Never put target markets, customer names, pricing or strategy in it. What goes into your actual decks and notebooks is private.
