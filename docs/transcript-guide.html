Working with EDEA Transcript

How a recorded meeting becomes two pages in the Brain, and how Claude finds what the meeting changes in pages we already have.

What it is

EDEA Transcript is our own meeting transcriber. This plugin files its meetings into the Brain.

EDEA Transcript records online calls and room meetings, and takes phone recordings by upload. Each meeting gets a transcript where every line has a speaker and a time. You name unknown voices on its review screen. The web app doesn't file anything — filing happens in Claude.

The plugin does two things with a meeting:

  • File it — a notes page in the house template, plus the full transcript on a child page, in the right space's Meetings tree.
  • Check the Brain against it — read the whole conversation, find what it decided or changed, and propose edits to the pages it makes out of date. Nothing is changed without your yes.

Getting connected

Once per person. No credentials or addresses live in the repo.

/plugin install edea-confluence@edea
/plugin install edea-transcript@edea

An admin adds it for the whole team in Claude's organization settings; a developer can add it by its address in Claude Code. The first use asks you to sign in with your staff Google account.

Names

The transcript names people correctly. The notes page still names nobody.

Every name has a source: your own microphone, a voice profile, or a person. The system never guesses. But the notes page body names nobody — that house rule stays.

  • Unnamed voice? Claude stops and asks. Name it on the review screen, where you can listen to a clip, or type the name in chat. If nobody knows, it stays "Unnamed voice 1".
  • The transcript page shows every line's speaker. That is its job.
  • The notes page lists participants once, in the details table, and phrases the rest as the room: "the team agreed", "one view was…".
  • Task owners come from you at filing time, never from who spoke.

Filing a meeting

Say it your way — "file my last meeting", "add yesterday's call to the Brain" — or paste the meeting's link.

  1. Claude finds the meeting

    From the link, or from the recent meetings list. If more than one fits, it asks. A meeting still processing can't be filed yet.

  2. Reads all of it

    The whole transcript, first line to last, before writing a word.

  3. Settles the names

    Any unnamed voice is named first.

  4. Asks three questions

    • Which space?
    • Hebrew or English? — for the notes page. The transcript keeps the words as spoken.
    • Task owners? — name them, or leave them unowned.

  5. Shows the drafts, files on your yes

    The notes page in full; the transcript as its outline and first lines, with the rest on request. Notes page first, then the transcript as its child. The meeting is marked filed in the web app, with links to both pages.

  6. Checks the Brain

    Then it moves straight to the Brain check below. You can skip it.

The two pages

Both sit in the space's Meetings tree.

PageHolds
NotesThe eight-section Meeting notes template: details · summary · main points · decisions · open questions · action items · next steps · related. Titled YYYY-MM-DD — subject. Links back to the meeting in the web app, where the audio is.
TranscriptEvery line, in order: [00:03:12] Speaker: words. Nothing cut or reworded. A very long meeting is split into parts by time.

Neither page is ever rewritten later. Meeting notes are records. Wrong wording in the transcript is fixed on the Confluence page.

The Brain check

A meeting often changes something the Brain already says. This finds it.

Claude proposes; you decide. Every edit is shown before → after and waits for your yes. You can say "that was not a decision", and that is final.

  1. Reads the conversation, not just the summary

    Every point that settles, changes or drops something — a rule, a number, a date, a tool, a scope. Side remarks and the last five minutes count. When a subject comes back later in the meeting, the last word wins.

  2. Marks each point settled or discussed

    Settled means the room arrived at it. One person proposing it is discussed.

  3. Finds what the Brain says

    Decision rules pages first, then a search by subject in Hebrew and English.

  4. Shows one report

    For each point: the quote and its time, the page sentence it touches, and the proposed edit with anything it ripples to.

The meeting…Claude proposes
Changed a ruleThe Decision page edited in place, old rule under Rejected, a dated Changed line
Dropped an ideaOne Rejected entry, and every other mention removed
Decided something newA rules line, or a Decision page when something was rejected
Made a Spec or Runbook wrongThat page edited, before → after
Only discussed itA flag and a suggested open question — no edit
Disagrees with ResearchA flag — no edit unless you ask

Safety checks on every proposal

  • If the page changed after the meeting, Claude flags it instead — an old meeting never rolls the Brain back.
  • If the edit rests on one number or name the transcriber heard, Claude asks you to confirm it first.
  • If guests were in the room, or the subject belongs to people who weren't, Claude says so.

Run it alone on a meeting you filed earlier: "what does last Tuesday's meeting change in the Brain?"

The skills

What the plugin installs.

SkillJob
transcript-fileFiles a meeting: reads all of it, settles names, asks space, language and owners, writes the nameless notes and the transcript page, files both on your yes, then starts the Brain check.
transcript-brain-checkFinds what a meeting changes in the Brain and proposes each edit before → after. Writes only what you approve.

Both lean on TRANSCRIPT.md, the shared reference inside the plugin, and on the edea-confluence rules rather than their own copy.

The skills tell Claude how to work; this page tells people. If they ever disagree, the skill is what's actually running — fix the page.
